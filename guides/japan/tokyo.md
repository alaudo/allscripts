## Orientation

Tokyo is a metropolis of linked centres rather than a single downtown.

Most first visits work best when organised by rail corridors and neighbourhood clusters.

Arriving from Hakone, you will usually enter via Shinjuku on the Odakyu Romancecar or via Tokyo/Shinagawa after changing from Odawara.

Tokyo Station is best for Shinkansen, Marunouchi, Ginza and Imperial Palace access.

Shinjuku is best for nightlife, west-side hotels, buses and easy access from Hakone-Yumoto.

Ueno suits museums, older neighbourhoods and trains towards northern Japan.

Asakusa gives temple atmosphere and Sumida River access, with Tokyo Skytree nearby.

Shibuya, Harajuku and Omotesando form a west-side youth culture, fashion and design cluster.

Ginza, Tsukiji, Toyosu and the bay area form an east and waterfront cluster.

The JR Yamanote line is the useful mental map for many major centres.

Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway fill the gaps and often beat the Yamanote for cross-town trips.

Suica and Pasmo cards are the simplest way to ride trains, buses and buy small items.

Three nights gives a fast first visit after the Kansai and Hakone legs.

Five nights or more makes room for museums, neighbourhood wandering and a day trip.

Hotel location matters more than hotel size; choose a station with easy transfers.

Avoid changing hotel every night inside Tokyo unless you have a clear reason.

Rush hour is real, especially with luggage, so travel light and avoid peak commuting when possible.

Restaurant queues can be part of the trip, but Tokyo also rewards spontaneous side streets.

Book timed-entry attractions, major observation decks and popular digital art museums ahead where required.

For departure, Tokyo has rail links to both Haneda and Narita; choose accommodation with that final transfer in mind.

## 101 — first visit

See the headline contrasts: temple Tokyo, modern crossings, gardens, food streets and one big view.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Asakusa and Sensoji | Tokyo's classic temple district with gates, stalls and backstreets. | 2-3 hours | Go early for atmosphere before tour groups build. |
| Tokyo Skytree | Tall observation tower and clear landmark across the Sumida. | 1.5-3 hours | Book or check current ticketing; views depend on weather. |
| Shibuya crossing and Shibuya Sky | Iconic street scene plus a polished skyline view. | 1.5-3 hours | Reserve Shibuya Sky when possible. |
| Shinjuku | Neon, food alleys, transport energy and observatory options. | 3-5 hours | Omoide Yokocho and Golden Gai are compact but crowded; the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatories are a useful free-view option when open. |
| Meiji Jingu | Forest shrine beside Harajuku's commercial bustle. | 1-2 hours | Best paired with Harajuku and Omotesando. |
| Imperial Palace East Gardens | Calm central gardens on former Edo Castle grounds. | 1-2 hours | Check current opening days before going. |
| Ginza | Department stores, design, stationery, dining and Kabukiza nearby. | 2-4 hours | Good in the afternoon and evening. |
| Ueno Park and Tokyo National Museum | Major museums, park life and seasonal blossoms. | Half day | The museum can fill a whole visit. |
| Tsukiji Outer Market | Food stalls, knives, seafood snacks and breakfast energy. | 1.5-3 hours | Pair with Ginza or Hama-rikyu. |

### A one-day plan

- **08:00-10:30:** Start at Sensoji and Asakusa before the streets become too busy.

- **10:30-12:00:** Walk or ride towards Tokyo Skytree for a view if the weather is clear.

- **12:00-13:30:** Lunch in Asakusa, Ueno or around Tokyo Station depending on your route.

- **13:30-15:30:** Visit the Imperial Palace East Gardens or Tokyo National Museum.

- **15:30-17:30:** Continue to Ginza for shops, cafés, architecture and Kabukiza exterior views.

- **18:00-20:00:** Move to Shibuya for the crossing and Shibuya Sky if booked.

- **20:00 onwards:** Eat around Shibuya or ride to Shinjuku for Omoide Yokocho and a night view.

### Food to try

- Sushi ranges from casual standing counters to high-end reservations; do not assume every good meal is expensive.

- Ramen shops are everywhere, with styles from shoyu and shio to rich tonkotsu-inspired bowls.

- Soba is a practical lunch around station districts and older neighbourhoods.

- Tempura, tonkatsu, yakitori and curry rice are easy first-visit comfort choices.

- Depachika food halls beneath department stores are excellent for takeaway meals and sweets.

- Tsukiji Outer Market is useful for seafood snacks, tamagoyaki, knives and breakfast browsing.

- Convenience stores are genuinely useful for breakfast, drinks and late-night backup food.

- Izakaya are best with small groups willing to share dishes and order gradually.

### Practical notes

- Use Suica or Pasmo rather than buying single tickets for every ride.

- The Yamanote line is useful but not always fastest; check subway options too.

- Station exits matter: a wrong exit at Shinjuku, Tokyo or Shibuya can add a long walk.

- Book Shibuya Sky, teamLab and popular restaurants ahead when they matter to your plan.

- Carry a small rubbish bag; public bins are limited.

- Stand on the correct side of escalators according to local flow, and avoid blocking station paths.

- Many museums and gardens close on particular weekdays; check current hours.

- Late-night trains are not all-night trains; check your final connection if staying out.

## 201 — second visit

Move beyond the obvious nodes into neighbourhood texture, performance, gardens and specialist museums.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Yanaka | Low-rise lanes, temples, cats, crafts and old Tokyo mood. | 2-4 hours | Pair with Nezu Shrine or Ueno. |
| Kagurazaka | Sloped lanes, restaurants, shrines and a layered old entertainment district. | 2-4 hours | Good for dinner if you reserve. |
| Shimokitazawa | Vintage shops, small theatres, cafés and music venues. | 2-4 hours | Best as a wandering neighbourhood. |
| Nakameguro and Daikanyama | Canal walks, boutiques, bookshops and relaxed cafés. | 2-4 hours | Famous for cherry blossoms but pleasant off-season. |
| Kiyosumi Teien | Refined strolling garden east of the centre. | 1-2 hours | Pair with coffee in Kiyosumi-Shirakawa. |
| Rikugien | Edo-period garden with seasonal colour. | 1-2 hours | Check current hours and special openings. |
| Hama-rikyu | Tidal garden between towers and Tokyo Bay. | 1.5-2.5 hours | Works well after Tsukiji. |
| Kabuki at Kabukiza | Accessible way into traditional theatre in Ginza. | 1 act-half day | Check current performance format. |
| Sumo at Ryogoku | Sport, ritual and neighbourhood history. | 2 hours-half day | Tournament dates and tickets need advance checking. |

### A one-day plan

- **Morning:** Visit Yanaka, Nezu and Ueno for temples, lanes and a museum or park walk.

- **Lunch:** Eat around Ueno, Nezu or Nippori rather than rushing to a famous queue.

- **Afternoon:** Choose one garden: Rikugien for classic design, Kiyosumi Teien for east-side calm or Hama-rikyu for bay contrast.

- **Late afternoon:** Rest at the hotel or a kissaten-style café; Tokyo rewards pacing.

- **Evening:** Book Kabuki at Kabukiza, izakaya in Kagurazaka or a small-venue night in Shimokitazawa.

### Food and drink

- Seek kissaten for old-school coffee, toast and a slower rhythm.

- Try monjayaki if exploring the east side, especially around Tsukishima.

- Kagurazaka is strong for French-influenced dining, Japanese restaurants and back-lane bars.

- Koenji and Shimokitazawa are good for casual bars, live music and late meals.

- Department-store basements are still useful on a second visit; the quality and range are high.

- Toyosu is the modern wholesale market area; check rules, viewing options and current access before going.

- If booking sushi or kaiseki, learn the cancellation policy and arrive on time.

### Practical notes

- Build days around two or three neighbourhoods, not a dozen station hops.

- For popular gardens in blossom or foliage seasons, arrive early or accept crowds.

- Kabuki, sumo and major exhibitions have their own calendars; plan around dates, not vague intentions.

- Tokyo's quieter neighbourhoods are residential; keep noise low and avoid photographing people intrusively.

- Many small bars have cover charges or limited seating; read signs and be polite if turned away.

- If you enjoyed Hakone's ryokan calm, schedule a light Tokyo day rather than going straight to maximum intensity.

- Use lockers for day-trip luggage, but avoid assuming large lockers will be free at peak times.

## 301 — deep dive

Explore Tokyo through design, subcultures, seasonal rituals, peripheral museums and trips that reveal the region.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Edo-Tokyo Open Air Architectural Museum | Preserved buildings that explain everyday urban history. | Half day | Located in Koganei; check current hours. |
| Nezu Museum | Art, architecture and a garden in Aoyama. | 2-3 hours | Pair with Omotesando or 21_21 Design Sight. |
| 21_21 Design Sight | Compact design exhibitions in Roppongi area. | 1-2 hours | Check the current exhibition before going. |
| Mori Art Museum | Contemporary art high above Roppongi. | 2-3 hours | Often combines well with a city view. |
| Akihabara | Electronics, games, anime culture and niche retail. | 2-4 hours | Go with curiosity and realistic expectations. |
| Koenji | Second-hand shops, music, bars and neighbourhood festivals. | 2-4 hours | Evening is livelier than morning. |
| teamLab | Immersive digital art experience. | 2-3 hours | Book timed entry and check the current venue details. |
| Toyosu and bay islands | Markets, waterfronts, large-scale museums and new developments. | Half day | Distances are larger than they look. |
| Day trip to Nikko | Shrines, forest and mountain history north of Tokyo. | Full day-long day | Start early and check transport reservations. |
| Day trip to Kamakura | Temples, sea air and easy walking south of Tokyo. | Full day | Very popular in hydrangea and holiday seasons. |
| Mount Takao | Accessible hiking and views on Tokyo's western edge. | Half day-full day | Trails vary; wear suitable shoes. |
| Izu or Boso coasts | Seaside contrast after the city. | Full day-overnight | Choose carefully; travel times vary widely. |

### Themed itineraries

- **Design Tokyo:** Nezu Museum → Omotesando architecture → 21_21 Design Sight → Mori Art Museum.

- **Old-to-new east side:** Asakusa backstreets → Sumida riverfront → Ryogoku sumo area → Kiyosumi Teien → Toyosu waterfront.

- **Neighbourhood culture west:** Nakameguro → Daikanyama → Shibuya backstreets → Shimokitazawa → Koenji evening.

### Seasonal and festival calendar

| Month/Season | What happens | Booking note |
|---|---|---|
| Late winter | Plum blossoms and clearer skyline days. | Observation decks still depend on weather. |
| Late March-April | Cherry blossoms in Ueno, Chidorigafuchi, Meguro River and many local parks. | Hotels and restaurants book quickly. |
| May | Kanda Matsuri occurs in odd-numbered years and Sanja Matsuri brings huge crowds to Asakusa. | Check exact annual dates and book central hotels early. |
| June | Rainy season begins; hydrangeas are good for garden and temple visits. | Keep indoor museum backups. |
| July | Sumida River fireworks usually draw major crowds. | Transport and riverside viewing require patience. |
| August | Heat and humidity shape the day; evenings are better for wandering. | Choose hotels near useful stations. |
| September-October | Typhoon risk, exhibitions and comfortable late-season evenings. | Keep plans flexible around storms. |
| November-early December | Autumn colour in gardens such as Rikugien and Koishikawa Korakuen. | Special openings may need checking. |
| New Year period | Shrines are busy and some businesses close. | Confirm opening days for everything. |

### Practical notes

- Tokyo rewards repeat visits because every rail line has its own set of neighbourhoods.

- Save exact restaurant hunts for meals that truly matter; otherwise let the nearest good side street work.

- For day trips, start early enough to avoid turning the destination into a commute-only exercise.

- Nikko needs a long day; Kamakura is easier and more flexible.

- Mount Takao is accessible but still a hike, not a shopping street.

- The Izu and Boso coasts can be excellent, but check train times and avoid overambitious same-day loops.

- Museums in Roppongi, Ueno and Koganei can have closure days, installation periods and timed exhibitions.

- For teamLab, confirm the current location, entry process and clothing advice before going.

- If you like architecture, include both polished districts and older wooden neighbourhoods for contrast.

- If you like music, check Koenji, Shimokitazawa and Shibuya listings, but do not assume English information is complete.

- Keep one unscheduled evening; Tokyo's best memories often come from a short walk after dinner.

- Keep a saved offline map area because station interiors and underground exits can be disorienting.

- Learn the neighbourhood name and nearest station exit for your hotel in Japanese or romaji.

- If you use taxis, have the destination written down; trains are usually faster for cross-town travel.

- Do not plan every meal as a reservation, or the city will feel like a timetable.

- For small restaurants, avoid lingering after finishing when people are waiting outside.

- If a queue has a ticket machine, watch the local order of operations before joining confidently.

- Some shops and restaurants are upstairs or downstairs with modest signs; look beyond street level.

- Department stores are useful for toilets, food, gifts and a calm reset in bad weather.

- Station bakeries and rice-ball shops make early starts much easier.

- In summer, plan indoor breaks between outdoor neighbourhood walks.

- In winter, clear days can be excellent for observation decks, but evenings get cold quickly.

- For cherry blossoms, choose one famous place and one local canal or park to avoid fatigue.

- For autumn colour, gardens are more reliable than random city streets.

- If a major museum exhibition is the priority, book it before arranging the surrounding day.

- Ueno museums can absorb more time than expected, especially the Tokyo National Museum.

- Roppongi works best when treated as art, design and dining rather than only nightlife.

- Akihabara is more enjoyable if you know what subculture or shop type you are looking for.

- Harajuku is busiest on weekends; Omotesando feels more spacious but still crowded.

- Shinjuku's west side, east side and south side feel like different districts; pick your exit carefully.

- Shibuya construction and redevelopment can change pedestrian routes, so follow current signs.

- Asakusa is not only Sensoji; leave time for quieter streets away from Nakamise.

- Ginza is good for window-shopping even if you do not intend to buy anything.

- Toyosu and Odaiba need more transit time than a central map suggests.

- If you are continuing elsewhere in Japan, Tokyo Station and Shinagawa are the key Shinkansen anchors.

- If Tokyo is your final stop, keep the last evening simple and pack before a morning airport transfer.
