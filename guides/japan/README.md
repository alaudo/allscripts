# Japan travel guide — Fukuoka to Tokyo

> A west-to-east route through Japan: Fukuoka → Hiroshima → Himeji → Kinosaki Onsen → Osaka → Hakone → Tokyo.
> Each stop has three guide levels (101, 201, 301) and a self-guided walking route.

These guides are travel companions to [allScripts](../../index.html), which teaches the kana used on
every station sign, menu, and ticket machine you will meet on this route.

The order is deliberate: it runs with the Shinkansen from Kyushu towards Tokyo, so you never
backtrack, and it puts the two slow, restorative stops (Kinosaki Onsen and Hakone) between the
busy city blocks.

### Legs between stops

| Leg | Usual way to travel | Rough journey time |
|---|---|---|
| Fukuoka (Hakata) → Hiroshima | Sanyo Shinkansen | ~1 hour |
| Hiroshima → Himeji | Sanyo Shinkansen | ~1 hour |
| Himeji → Kinosaki Onsen | JR Bantan or Hamakaze limited express via Wadayama | ~2–2.5 hours |
| Kinosaki Onsen → Osaka | Kinosaki limited express to Osaka, or via Kyoto | ~2.5–3 hours |
| Osaka → Hakone | Tokaido Shinkansen to Odawara, then Hakone Tozan line/bus | ~3 hours |
| Hakone → Tokyo | Odakyu Romancecar from Hakone-Yumoto, or Shinkansen from Odawara | ~1.5 hours / ~35 minutes |

Check current timetables before travelling; limited express services on the Sea of Japan side run
far less frequently than the Shinkansen and are worth reserving in advance.

## How the levels work

Every city guide is split into three levels. They are cumulative, not alternatives: level 201
assumes you have already done the 101 list on an earlier trip or an earlier day.

| Level | Who it is for | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| **101 — first visit** | First time in the city, one or two days, limited time | The essential sights, one workable day plan, the food the city is known for |
| **201 — second visit** | Extra days, or a return trip | Second-tier sights, neighbourhoods, day trips, quieter versions of the famous places |
| **301 — deep dive** | Repeat visitors, long stays, special interests | Specialist museums and crafts, hikes and islands, architecture, festivals and season-dependent experiences, themed itineraries |

Each guide also has a **Language pointers** table with words you will actually read or say in that
city, and a linked **walking guide** with a step-by-step route.

## Planning notes for the whole trip

### Rail passes and tickets

- A nationwide JR pass covers most of this route (Shinkansen except Nozomi/Mizuho services, plus
  the limited expresses to and from Kinosaki Onsen), but not the Odakyu Romancecar, the Hakone
  ropeway and boats, Osaka's subway, or Hiroshima's trams.
- Regional passes (Sanyo–San'in, Kansai Wide, Hakone Freepass) can beat a nationwide pass if you
  are moving slowly. Price the route both ways before buying.
- Carry a Suica, Pasmo, or ICOCA IC card for local trains, trams, buses, lockers, and convenience
  stores. All the major cards work interchangeably across the regions on this route.
- Reserve Shinkansen seats when travelling with large luggage: oversized baggage spaces on the
  Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen must be booked with the seat.

### Luggage

- Use luggage forwarding (*takkyubin*) for the awkward legs, especially Himeji → Kinosaki Onsen and
  the Hakone stay, where you will be changing trains and walking to a ryokan. Send bags a day ahead
  and travel with an overnight bag.
- Coin lockers exist at every station on this route, but the large sizes fill up by mid-morning at
  Hiroshima, Himeji, and Hakone-Yumoto.

### When to go

| Season | What it is like | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (Mar–May) | Cherry blossom at Himeji Castle and Osaka Castle, mild walking weather | Golden Week (late April to early May) books out and prices spike |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Festivals, long daylight, Sea of Japan beaches near Kinosaki | Rainy season in June, serious heat and humidity in July and August |
| Autumn (Sep–Nov) | The best all-round season: clear air, autumn colour in Hakone and Miyajima | Typhoons into September; autumn colour weekends are crowded |
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Crab season in Kinosaki, empty temples, crisp Fuji views from Hakone | Snow can disrupt the San'in line; many gardens look bare |

### Etiquette that applies everywhere on this route

- Onsen: wash thoroughly before entering the bath, no swimwear, keep the small towel out of the
  water. Some baths still restrict tattoos — ask, or look for private or family baths.
- Eat and drink where you bought it rather than while walking, especially in market streets such as
  Kuromon Ichiba and Nishiki-style arcades.
- Queue at the marked positions on station platforms, keep phone calls off trains, and take your
  rubbish with you: public bins are rare.
- Cash still matters at small ryokan, public baths, shrines, and rural buses, even though cities are
  now largely cashless.

### Suggested shapes for the trip

- **10 days:** Fukuoka 2, Hiroshima 2, Himeji 1 (day stop), Kinosaki 1, Osaka 2, Tokyo 2.
- **14 days:** as above with a second Kinosaki night, a Hakone night, and two more Tokyo nights.
- **21 days:** run 101 and 201 in each city, add Osaka's day trips and Tokyo's 301 themed days.

## Learning the script as you go

Station names, bath signs, and menus on this route are written in kanji with kana furigana, and the
kana alone will get you a long way: 入口 / いりぐち (entrance), 出口 / でぐち (exit), 女 (women),
男 (men), 湯 (hot water). Work through the hiragana deck in
[allScripts](../../index.html) before the trip and the signs in these guides will start to read
themselves.
