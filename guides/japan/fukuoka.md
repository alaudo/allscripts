# Fukuoka — travel guide

## Orientation

Fukuoka sits on the north coast of Kyushu, facing the Korea Strait.

The city is unusually convenient: Fukuoka Airport is only a short subway ride from Hakata Station.

Hakata Station is the main rail hub for shinkansen services, JR local lines and many buses.

Tenjin is the main shopping, nightlife and private-rail hub.

Most first visits work best with two nights.

One night covers ramen, temples and a park walk; three nights allow a proper day trip.

From a westbound or international arrival, come in via Fukuoka Airport or the Sanyo-Kyushu Shinkansen to Hakata.

Leave for Hiroshima by shinkansen from Hakata; reserve seats if travelling at busy holiday times.

Within the city, use the subway, buses and walking.

IC cards such as Hayakaken, Sugoca, Nimoca, Suica and ICOCA are widely usable on local transport.

A nationwide JR Pass is only good value if your wider itinerary includes enough long-distance rail.

For city-only days, an IC card is simpler than a pass.

The useful layout is compact:

- Hakata: station, temples, business hotels and easy rail access.
- Gion and Nakasu: old-town shrines, canals, nightlife and yatai areas.
- Tenjin: department stores, underground shopping and evening transport.
- Ohori and Maizuru: parkland, castle ruins and museums.
- Momochi and the bay: beaches, tower views and modern waterfront sights.

Good bases depend on the rhythm of your trip:

- Stay near Hakata if you have early trains, airport transfers or many day trips.
- Stay near Tenjin if food, shopping and nightlife matter more than rail logistics.
- Stay around Nakasu or Gion if you want to walk between the old town and evening stalls.
- Stay near Ohori only if you prefer parks and do not mind subway rides for dinner.
- Avoid splitting a short Fukuoka stay between hotels; transport is too easy to justify it.

Useful arrival and departure notes:

- Airport arrivals are simplest by subway to Hakata or Tenjin.
- Shinkansen arrivals should use Hakata as the first orientation point.
- Ferries and international services use port areas that need extra local transfers.
- The Hiroshima-bound shinkansen leaves from Hakata, not Tenjin.
- Seat reservations are sensible for the Fukuoka to Hiroshima leg during peak periods.
- If using luggage delivery, send bags to Hiroshima before a day trip rather than carrying them.
- Keep an IC card topped up; buses are useful for places the subway does not reach.

## 101 — first visit

Start with the city’s food, old Hakata core and the green belt around Ohori Park.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Hakata Station area | Arrival point, food floors and easy logistics | 30-90 minutes | Good first stop for lockers, rail reservations and a meal |
| Kushida Shrine | Hakata’s neighbourhood shrine and Yamakasa focus | 30-45 minutes | Be respectful around worshippers and festival displays |
| Tochoji | Large wooden seated Buddha and old temple atmosphere | 30-60 minutes | Check current access details for the Buddha hall |
| Shofukuji | Japan’s first Zen temple, valued for quiet precincts | 30-45 minutes | Buildings are usually viewed from outside |
| Canal City Hakata | Urban canal mall, restaurants and easy bad-weather stop | 45-90 minutes | Useful between Hakata and Nakasu |
| Ohori Park | Fukuoka’s classic pond walk and picnic space | 60-90 minutes | Combine with Maizuru Park and the castle ruins |
| Fukuoka Castle ruins | Stone walls, viewpoints and seasonal flowers | 45-90 minutes | Best with comfortable shoes |
| Tenjin | Shopping, cafés, nightlife and transport connections | 60-120 minutes |Underground mall navigation can save rainy-day walking |
| Yatai food stalls | Fukuoka’s signature open-air eating scene | 60-120 minutes | Go with patience; check current operating days and locations |

### A one-day plan

- **08:30-09:30 — Hakata breakfast and logistics.**
  Store luggage at Hakata Station if needed.
- **09:30-11:30 — Gion temples and shrines.**
  Walk to Tochoji, Shofukuji and Kushida Shrine.
- **11:30-12:30 — Canal City.**
  Use it for lunch, coffee or a weather break.
- **13:00-15:30 — Ohori Park and Fukuoka Castle ruins.**
  Circle part of the pond, then climb through Maizuru Park.
- **15:30-17:30 — Tenjin.**
  Browse department-store food halls, bookshops and the underground shopping streets.
- **18:00-20:00 — Tonkotsu ramen.**
  Eat early if you dislike queues.
- **20:00-late — Nakasu or Tenjin yatai.**
  Treat the stalls as a light meal or second dinner.

### Food to try

- Tonkotsu ramen (tonkotsu rāmen / とんこつラーメン): pork-bone broth with thin noodles.
- Hakata ramen (Hakata rāmen / 博多ラーメン): the local tonkotsu style, often with kaedama noodle refills.
- Motsunabe (motsunabe / もつ鍋): hotpot with beef or pork offal, cabbage and garlic chives.
- Mentaiko (mentaiko / 明太子): spicy cod roe, often served with rice, pasta or as a snack.
- Goma saba (goma saba / ごまさば): mackerel with sesame sauce, a Fukuoka favourite when fresh fish is available.
- Mizutaki (mizutaki / 水炊き): chicken hotpot with clear broth.
- Yakitori (yakitori / 焼き鳥): skewers; Fukuoka counters often serve cabbage alongside.
- Umegae mochi (umegae mochi / 梅ヶ枝餅): grilled rice cake associated with Dazaifu.
- Hakata udon (Hakata udon / 博多うどん): soft noodles with simple broth, a quieter local staple.
- Sesame-dressed fish (goma-ae no sakana / ごま和えの魚): ask what is fresh if goma saba is unavailable.

### Practical notes

- Reserve shinkansen seats for the Fukuoka to Hiroshima leg during holidays and weekends.
- Check current opening hours for temple halls, museums and yatai locations before setting out.
- Hakata Station has many coin lockers, but large lockers fill early on busy days.
- Fukuoka Airport is close, so do not overestimate transfer time, but still allow for luggage and ticketing.
- Many small ramen shops use ticket machines; keep cash as well as cards.
- At yatai, order something per person and avoid lingering when others are waiting.
- Do not photograph people at stalls or shrines without permission.
- In ramen shops, quick turnover is normal; eat, thank the staff and move on.
- Many temple and shrine areas are close to residential streets; keep luggage and noise controlled.
- For parks and day trips, carry a small rubbish bag; public bins can be limited.
- If you plan yatai after dinner, think of it as a social snack stop, not a long restaurant booking.

## 201 — second visit

Use extra time for day trips, seaside neighbourhoods and Fukuoka’s craft traditions.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Dazaifu Tenmangu | Major Tenjin shrine with a lively approach street | Half day | Often paired with Kyushu National Museum |
| Kyushu National Museum | Big-picture Asian and Japanese history | 2-3 hours | Check current exhibitions and closed days |
| Nanzoin | Reclining Buddha and hillside temple complex | Half day | Take the train to Kidonanzoin-mae; dress for slopes |
| Momochi Seaside Park | Beachfront, modern skyline and Fukuoka Tower area | 2-3 hours | Best in clear weather |
| Hakata Machiya Folk Museum | Compact introduction to Hakata life and crafts | 45-90 minutes | Good near Kushida Shrine |
| Yanagawa | Canal boats and old castle-town atmosphere | Day trip | Check boat schedules and weather |
| Itoshima | Coast, cafés, beaches and shrine scenery | Half or full day | A car helps, but some areas work by train and bus |
| Karatsu | Castle views, ceramics and coastal Saga culture | Day trip | Convenient by rail from Fukuoka |
| Munakata Taisha | Important shrine complex linked to maritime worship | Day trip | Public transport is possible but needs planning |

### A one-day plan

- **08:30-09:30 — Train to Dazaifu.**
  Start early to see the shrine approach before it gets crowded.
- **09:30-11:30 — Dazaifu Tenmangu.**
  Visit the main shrine area, side paths and plum-related sweets shops.
- **11:30-12:30 — Lunch near the approach.**
  Try umegae mochi and a simple noodle or set meal.
- **12:30-15:00 — Kyushu National Museum.**
  Focus on the permanent collection and one special exhibition if open.
- **15:00-16:30 — Return to central Fukuoka.**
  Break in Tenjin or Hakata.
- **17:30-19:30 — Motsunabe dinner.**
  Book ahead at popular restaurants.
- **20:00-21:30 — Nakasu riverside walk.**
  Finish with lights, stalls or dessert rather than another big meal.

### Food and drink

- Motsunabe is best shared; check whether the restaurant expects a minimum order.
- Mentaiko appears in rice bowls, omelettes, onigiri and souvenirs.
- Goma saba depends on fish quality and season; ask what is recommended that day.
- Dazaifu’s umegae mochi is best eaten warm from the griddle.
- Look for local sake from Fukuoka and Saga if you drink alcohol.
- Coffee culture is strong around Tenjin, Daimyo and Yakuin.
- Department-store basements are useful for picnic food for Ohori Park.

### Practical notes

- Dazaifu is popular with school groups, shrine visitors and exam-season worshippers.
- Kyushu National Museum and Dazaifu Tenmangu can be busy on weekends.
- Nanzoin paths include steps and slopes; avoid rushing the visit.
- Itoshima is easier with a rental car or carefully planned bus connections.
- Yanagawa boat trips are weather-dependent; check current operations.
- Munakata Taisha’s wider sacred-island context is culturally important, but access to Okinoshima itself is not for casual tourism.
- For crafts, verify workshop, museum and shop hours; small places may close irregularly.
- Use coin lockers at Hakata or Tenjin before day trips if changing hotels.
- For Dazaifu, check both train route and return timing before entering the museum.
- On coastal days, wind can matter as much as rain; keep plans flexible.
- If visiting craft shops, small purchases are often easier to pack than framed or fragile pieces.

## 301 — deep dive

Return visitors can follow Fukuoka through festivals, textiles, ceramics, islands and the wider history of Hakata as a port.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Hakata Gion Yamakasa festival | High-energy July festival central to Hakata identity | Seasonal | Check current dates, viewing rules and transport changes |
| Hakata ori textiles | Silk weaving tradition used for obi and accessories | 1-2 hours | Look for demonstrations, shops or museum displays |
| Hakata dolls | Painted clay figures with a long local craft history | 1-2 hours | Studios and displays vary; check current access |
| Nokonoshima | Island flowers, bay views and slower pace | Half day | Ferry and bus timings matter |
| Shikanoshima | Coastal cycling, beaches and ancient history links | Half or full day | Weather and wind shape the day |
| Genko Historical Museum area | Context for the Mongol invasions and defensive walls | 1-2 hours | Combine with the western waterfront |
| Karatsu ceramics trail | Pottery, castle town and festival culture | Full day | Good for craft-focused travellers |
| Munakata and Fukutsu coast | Shrines, sea views and old maritime routes | Full day | Plan transport carefully |
| Yanagawa literary and water-town walk | Canals, old houses and regional food | Full day | Slower than a checklist day |

### Themed itineraries

- **Hakata craft day:** Hakata Machiya Folk Museum → Kushida Shrine area → Hakata ori shop or display → Hakata doll gallery or studio → Tenjin design shops.
- **Bay and island day:** Meinohama ferry terminal → Nokonoshima → island flower park or village lanes → return for Momochi sunset.
- **Port history day:** Hakata old town → Tochoji → Shofukuji exterior → Genko defensive-wall sites → Fukuoka City Museum area.
- **Festival context day:** Kushida Shrine → decorated kazariyama float display when available → old Hakata lanes → local craft shops → yatai dinner.
- **Northern Kyushu rail day:** Fukuoka → Karatsu → pottery and castle views → return via coastal scenery.

### Seasonal and festival calendar

| Month/Season | What happens | Booking note |
|---|---|---|
| January | Shrine visits and winter food such as hotpot | Reserve popular restaurants around New Year |
| February-March | Plum blossoms at Dazaifu Tenmangu | Expect crowds in good bloom weeks |
| March-April | Cherry blossoms around Maizuru Park and Ohori Park | Hotels can fill on peak weekends |
| May | Warm weather for Itoshima and bay walks | Check bus schedules before coastal trips |
| July | Hakata Gion Yamakasa reaches its climax | Book accommodation early and check street closures |
| August | Hot, humid evenings; beaches and riverside food | Hydrate and plan indoor breaks |
| September-October | Comfortable walking, craft browsing and day trips | Good season for longer walks |
| November | Autumn colours in parks and temple areas | Check foliage reports, not fixed dates |
| December | Illuminations around Hakata and Tenjin | Evening areas are busy but manageable |

### Practical notes

- Festival viewing etiquette matters: follow barriers, police instructions and local guidance.
- During Hakata Gion Yamakasa, some streets and transport patterns change early in the morning.
- Craft workshops may require advance booking and may not operate in English.
- For island trips, check the last ferry before leaving the mainland.
- Weather changes quickly on the coast; carry layers outside summer.
- Summer heat makes mid-day walking tiring; shift walks to morning and evening.
- Regional trains can be less frequent than city subways; confirm return times.
- If buying ceramics or textiles, ask about packing for travel.
- Use takuhaibin luggage delivery if combining Fukuoka with several one-night stops.
