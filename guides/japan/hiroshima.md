## Orientation

Hiroshima lies in western Honshu on the Seto Inland Sea.

Hiroshima Station is the shinkansen gateway and the main rail hub.

The Peace Memorial Park area is west of the station beside the Motoyasu and Honkawa rivers.

Most visitors arrive from Fukuoka by shinkansen from Hakata to Hiroshima.

Leave east for Himeji, Osaka or Kyoto by shinkansen from Hiroshima Station.

Two nights is the practical minimum for the Peace Memorial area and Miyajima.

Three nights allow a slower museum visit, local food and a day trip such as Onomichi or Iwakuni.

The city is famous for its tram network, operated by Hiroden.

Trams are slower than a subway but useful, visible and easy to understand.

IC cards are widely usable on trams, buses and many local trains.

For Miyajima, combine JR or Hiroden transport with the ferry from Miyajimaguchi.

Check whether any rail pass covers your ferry and train choices before buying separate tickets.

The useful layout is simple:

- Hiroshima Station: shinkansen, hotels, buses and food floors.
- Hatchobori and Hondori: shopping arcades, restaurants and tram junctions.
- Peace Memorial Park: museum, memorials and riverside walking.
- Shukkei-en and Hiroshima Castle: garden, history and calmer central sights.
- Miyajima: shrine island, Mt Misen and a full-day excursion.

Good bases depend on what you want to prioritise:

- Stay near Hiroshima Station if you are using the shinkansen, Mazda Museum or day trips.
- Stay near Hatchobori or Hondori if dinners, trams and shopping arcades are the focus.
- Stay near the Peace Memorial Park if you want to walk to memorial sites early or late.
- Stay on Miyajima only if you want the island after day crowds leave.
- Do not move hotels within central Hiroshima for a short stay; the tram network makes that unnecessary.

Useful arrival and departure notes:

- From Fukuoka, the shinkansen trip begins at Hakata and ends at Hiroshima Station.
- For Himeji, Osaka or Kyoto, return to Hiroshima Station for the fastest trains.
- Tram stops outside the station can be busy; allow extra minutes when carrying bags.
- Buses can be quicker for some central journeys, but trams are easier for first-time visitors.
- Miyajima needs a train or tram to Miyajimaguchi, then a ferry.
- Keep your return ferry and train timing in mind if staying in central Hiroshima.
- If you have a rail pass, confirm exactly which Miyajima ferry services it covers.

## 101 — first visit

Begin with the Peace Memorial area, then use Miyajima and local food to understand the city beyond one story.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Peace Memorial Park | Central memorial landscape and essential orientation | 1-2 hours | Move quietly and allow time to pause |
| Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum | Core historical context for the atomic bombing | 2-3 hours | Check current hours and consider emotional intensity |
| A-Bomb Dome | Preserved ruin and UNESCO-listed symbol | 20-40 minutes | Best understood with the park and museum |
| Cenotaph and Flame of Peace | Main memorial axis through the park | 15-30 minutes | Avoid blocking visitors who are paying respects |
| Shukkei-en | Historic garden rebuilt after the war | 45-90 minutes | Pleasant before or after the museum |
| Hiroshima Castle | Reconstructed castle keep and moat setting | 60-90 minutes | Check current exhibition access |
| Hondori arcade | Central covered shopping and easy food break | 30-90 minutes | Useful in rain or heat |
| Miyajima and Itsukushima Shrine | Shrine, torii views and island atmosphere | Half or full day | Tide changes affect the view; check current shrine works |
| Mt Misen | Island summit views and forest paths | 2-5 hours | Ropeway and hiking options depend on weather |

### A one-day plan

- **08:30-09:00 — Tram or bus to Peace Memorial Park.**
  Start before the area becomes crowded.
- **09:00-11:30 — Peace Memorial Museum.**
  Take breaks if needed; the exhibits are heavy.
- **11:30-12:30 — Park memorials and A-Bomb Dome.**
  Walk the main axis and riverside paths.
- **12:30-13:30 — Lunch near Hondori.**
  Choose okonomiyaki or a lighter café meal.
- **14:00-15:30 — Shukkei-en.**
  Reset in the garden after the morning’s memorial sites.
- **15:45-17:00 — Hiroshima Castle.**
  Walk the moat and castle grounds.
- **18:00-20:00 — Hiroshima okonomiyaki.**
  Sit at a counter if available and watch the layered cooking.
- **20:00-21:00 — Riverside evening walk.**
  Return towards the Peace Park or station by tram.

### Food to try

- Hiroshima okonomiyaki (Hiroshima okonomiyaki / 広島お好み焼き): layered savoury pancake with noodles.
- Oysters (kaki / 牡蠣): grilled, fried, steamed or served in hotpot depending on season and restaurant.
- Anago meshi (anago meshi / 穴子飯): conger eel over rice, strongly associated with Miyajima.
- Tsukemen (Hiroshima tsukemen / 広島つけ麺): cold noodles dipped in spicy sauce.
- Momiji manju (momiji manjū / もみじ饅頭): maple-leaf-shaped cake from Miyajima.
- Lemon products (remon / レモン): Setouchi lemons appear in desserts, drinks and sauces.
- Sake from Hiroshima Prefecture: especially from Saijo if you have time for a side trip.
- Kaki fry (kaki furai / 牡蠣フライ): fried oysters, common in casual restaurants in season.
- Onomichi ramen (Onomichi rāmen / 尾道ラーメン): a soy-based regional ramen for an Onomichi day.

### Practical notes

- The Peace Memorial Museum can be crowded; check current ticketing and opening details.
- Do not rush the museum between trains; it deserves time and emotional space.
- Photography may be restricted in some areas; follow posted rules.
- Hiroshima Station and major tram stops have coin lockers, but large lockers can fill.
- Trams are convenient but not fast; allow buffer time for shinkansen connections.
- For Miyajima, check tide times if the torii gate view matters to you.
- Deer on Miyajima are wild animals; do not feed them or let them take paper.
- Many okonomiyaki counters are small; groups may need to split or wait.
- The museum shop and nearby bookshops can be useful for reliable English-language context.
- If you are emotionally affected after the museum, schedule quiet garden or riverside time.
- In summer, carry water before entering long museum queues or exposed memorial spaces.

## 201 — second visit

Add industrial history, manga culture, garden time and Setouchi day trips once the core memorial and Miyajima visits are done.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Mazda Museum | Hiroshima’s major industry story | Half day | Advance reservations are usually required; check current arrangements |
| Hiroshima City Manga Library | Specialist collection in a quiet park setting | 1-2 hours | Good for Japanese-reading visitors and manga fans |
| Mitaki-dera | Forested temple and hillside atmosphere | 1-2 hours | Paths include steps; go in daylight |
| Saijo sake district | Breweries, kura architecture and tastings | Half day | Check tasting rules, driver restrictions and opening days |
| Iwakuni Kintaikyo | Elegant wooden bridge and castle-town outing | Half or full day | Combine bridge, riverside and ropeway if operating |
| Onomichi | Temple walk, slopes and Inland Sea views | Full day | Also a gateway to the Shimanami Kaido |
| Shimanami Kaido | Cycling route across Setouchi islands | Full day or longer | Book bikes and luggage plans ahead in busy seasons |
| Tomonoura | Harbour town scenery linked to the Inland Sea | Full day | Slower by public transport; plan carefully |
| Okunoshima | Island known for rabbits and wartime remains | Full day | Do not feed inappropriate food; check ferry times |

### A one-day plan

- **08:30-10:00 — Train to Onomichi.**
  Travel light; station lockers are helpful if available.
- **10:00-12:30 — Temple Walk and slopes.**
  Follow viewpoints, lanes and small temples rather than trying to see everything.
- **12:30-13:30 — Lunch near the waterfront.**
  Try local ramen or simple seafood.
- **13:30-15:30 — Short Shimanami taste.**
  Rent a bike for a limited coastal section if weather and time allow.
- **15:30-17:00 — Coffee, shopping and return logistics.**
  Do not miss the return train you need.
- **18:30-20:00 — Hiroshima okonomiyaki or oysters.**
  Choose whichever you skipped on the first visit.
- **20:00-21:00 — Hondori or riverside walk.**
  Keep the evening easy after a long day trip.

### Food and drink

- Hiroshima okonomiyaki differs from Osaka style by layering batter, cabbage, fillings and noodles.
- Oyster season is strongest in cooler months, but preparations vary year-round.
- Miyajima’s anago meshi is a good lunch if you start early.
- Momiji manju makes an easy train snack; many fillings are available.
- Saijo is one of Japan’s best-known sake towns; drink slowly and plan transport.
- Setouchi lemons appear in soft drinks, cakes and savoury dishes.
- Onomichi ramen is a regional style worth trying on an Onomichi day.

### Practical notes

- Mazda Museum visits require current booking checks; do not assume walk-in entry.
- Shimanami cycling needs weather awareness, bike availability and a realistic distance plan.
- Onomichi slopes are charming but tiring with luggage.
- Iwakuni combines well with Miyajima only if you start early and keep priorities clear.
- Okunoshima ferries and buses require schedule checks, especially outside peak times.
- Tomonoura is rewarding but slower to reach than it looks on a map.
- Keep an IC card topped up; tram and local rail transfers are frequent.
- If travelling with children, balance the Peace Museum with outdoor time afterwards.
- For day trips, check last useful return trains rather than only the final train of the night.
- Island and gorge trips need cash more often than central Hiroshima does.
- If eating oysters, choose reputable restaurants and follow seasonal advice.

## 301 — deep dive

For longer stays, follow Hiroshima into islands, gorges, industry, literature and the wider Setouchi cultural landscape.

### Highlights

| Sight | Why go | Time needed | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Sandankyo Gorge | Forest, river scenery and seasonal walking | Full day | Check trail conditions and transport before going |
| Setouchi art islands | Contemporary art, ferries and island villages | 1-3 days | Requires careful ferry and accommodation planning |
| Dejima and Ujina port area | Modern waterfront, ferries and bay views | 2-4 hours | Good for a slower urban afternoon |
| Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum | Art collection beside Shukkei-en | 1-2 hours | Check current exhibitions |
| Former Bank of Japan Hiroshima Branch | Surviving historic building and exhibition space | 30-60 minutes | Access varies by exhibition schedule |
| Honkawa Elementary School Peace Museum | Local wartime memory in a school setting | 45-90 minutes | Check current access and be respectful |
| Etajima | Naval history, island roads and bay scenery | Full day | Transport planning is essential |
| Kure | Maritime and shipbuilding history near Hiroshima | Half or full day | Museum access and opening days vary |
| Saijo brewery architecture | Sake-town lanes and water sources | Half day | Best explored slowly and responsibly |

### Themed itineraries

- **Memory and reconstruction day:** Peace Memorial Museum → A-Bomb Dome → Honkawa area → Former Bank of Japan Hiroshima Branch → riverside reflection walk.
- **Garden and castle day:** Shukkei-en → Prefectural Art Museum → Hiroshima Castle moat → Central Park paths → okonomiyaki dinner.
- **Setouchi water day:** Hiroshima Port → ferry ride → Etajima or another bay island → seafood lunch → return by sunset.
- **Industry day:** Mazda Museum reservation → Hiroshima Station lunch → Kure maritime museums or port views → tram back for dinner.
- **Slow island day:** Miyajima back streets → Daisho-in → Mt Misen lower trails → late ferry after day crowds thin.

### Seasonal and festival calendar

| Month/Season | What happens | Booking note |
|---|---|---|
| January | Clear winter days, oysters and quieter memorial sites | Check holiday closures |
| February | Oyster dishes remain a strong draw | Book popular oyster restaurants on weekends |
| March-April | Cherry blossoms around rivers, parks and Miyajima | Accommodation prices can rise |
| May | Good cycling weather for the Shimanami Kaido | Reserve bikes early for holidays |
| June-July | Rainy season affects gorges, cycling and island ferries | Build flexible indoor alternatives |
| August | Peace Memorial Ceremony on 6 August | Book far ahead and expect solemn crowds |
| September-October | Comfortable walking and cycling season | Weekends are popular for Miyajima |
| November | Autumn colours on Miyajima and in gardens | Check foliage timing before committing |
| December | Illuminations and winter seafood | Keep evenings warm and transport simple |

### Practical notes

- Treat peace-related sites as memorial spaces, not just attractions.
- On 6 August, follow local instructions and expect ceremonies, road controls and heavy demand for accommodation.
- Some smaller peace museums and historic buildings have limited opening patterns.
- Ferries in the Setouchi region are scenic but unforgiving if you miss the last useful connection.
- Gorge walks and island hikes need proper footwear and weather checks.
- Summer heat and humidity are serious; carry water and schedule indoor breaks.
- If cycling the Shimanami Kaido, decide whether you are doing a sample section, a full crossing or an overnight ride.
- For art islands, verify which museums require timed tickets or close on certain weekdays.
- Keep cash for small island shops, temples and local buses.
