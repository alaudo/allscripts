# Hakone walking guide

> Old Tokaido cedar avenue, Hakone Checkpoint and Moto-Hakone lakeside; about 5-6 km, 2-3 hours, easy to moderate with short slopes and uneven old-road surfaces.

- **Start:** Hakone-machi-ko bus/boat area **Finish:** Moto-Hakone-ko **Distance:** ~5-6 km **Time:** 2-3 hours **Difficulty:** easy/moderate
- **City guide:** [Hakone](../hakone.md) · **Index:** [Japan guide index](../README.md)

1. Hakone-machi-ko lakeside arrival
2. Hakone Checkpoint approach

4. Lake Ashi viewpoint near Onshi-Hakone Park
5. Old Tokaido cedar avenue

9. Moto-Hakone-ko finish

## The walk

### 1. Hakone-machi-ko lakeside arrival

Start beside Lake Ashi where boats and buses connect with the wider Hakone Loop. The lake, ridges and weather give you an immediate sense of why this route mattered as both a checkpoint road and a scenic corridor.

**Next:** walk 5 minutes towards the checkpoint entrance.

### 2. Hakone Checkpoint approach

The path tightens into a more historical setting as you leave the modern transport stop. Look for views back over the lake before entering the reconstructed barrier-station area.

**Next:** walk 2 minutes into Hakone Checkpoint.

### 3. Hakone Checkpoint (Sekisho)

Hakone Checkpoint recalls the Edo-period controls on the Tokaido road between Kyoto and Edo. The reconstructed gates, lookout and exhibits help explain why this mountain pass was strategically important; check current hours before entering paid areas.

**Next:** walk 10 minutes towards Onshi-Hakone Park viewpoints.

### 4. Lake Ashi viewpoint near Onshi-Hakone Park

The park area gives some of the walk's broadest views over Lake Ashi. On a clear day Mount Fuji may appear beyond the water, but cloud often hides it completely.

**Next:** walk 10-15 minutes back towards the old road and cedar avenue.

### 5. Old Tokaido cedar avenue

This is the signature stretch: tall cedars lining a surviving section of the old highway. The atmosphere is strongest when mist, low cloud or late light softens the road.

**Next:** walk 15-20 minutes along the cedar avenue towards Moto-Hakone.

### 6. Moto-Hakone lakeside path

The route opens again to Lake Ashi with boats, small restaurants and shrine-bound visitors nearby. Take a break here if you want lunch before the shrine climb.

**Next:** walk 10 minutes along the lakeside towards the shrine approach.

### 7. Hakone Shrine approach

Stone lanterns and forest mark the shift from lakeside resort to shrine precinct. The climb is not long, but the steps can feel steeper after a day on Hakone transport.

**Next:** walk 5-10 minutes up to the main shrine buildings.

### 8. Hakone Shrine and lake torii

Hakone Shrine sits among cedars above the water and is one of Hakone's essential sights. The torii in the lake is popular for photographs, so expect queues at busy times and avoid blocking the path.

**Next:** walk 10-15 minutes back to Moto-Hakone-ko.

### 9. Moto-Hakone-ko finish

Finish at the boat and bus stop for onward travel around Lake Ashi, Hakone-Yumoto or Gora. If the weather has cleared, consider a final lake view before leaving.

**Next:** buses and boats connect with the wider Hakone Loop; check current schedules.

## Variations

- **Hakone Hachiri old road:** Extend onto longer preserved Tokaido sections towards Amazake-chaya and Hatajuku for a more serious old-road walk.

- **Amazake-chaya add-on:** Bus or walk to the historic teahouse for amazake and a stronger sense of the mountain route.

- **Reverse route:** Start at Moto-Hakone, visit the shrine early, then walk the cedar avenue to the checkpoint before crowds build.

- **Short version:** Skip Onshi-Hakone Park and focus on the checkpoint, cedar avenue and shrine.

- **Rainy-day version:** Keep the walk short, add longer museum or teahouse stops, and watch for slippery stone paving.

## Practical notes

- Wear shoes that can handle damp stone, roots and occasional mud.

- Check bus and boat times before starting, especially outside peak seasons.

- Mount Fuji views are a bonus, not a promise.

- The checkpoint and shrine areas can be busy after sightseeing boats arrive.

- Carry water and a layer; lake weather can change quickly.

- Respect shrine etiquette and keep noise low around worshippers.

- Do not continue onto longer old-road sections without checking daylight and route conditions.
