# Hiroshima walking guide

> A central Hiroshima route of about 6 km, taking 4-5 hours at an easy pace with serious museum time.

- **Start:** Hiroshima Station **Finish:** Hondori or Peace Memorial Park **Distance:** ~6 km **Time:** 4-5 hours **Difficulty:** easy
- **City guide:** [Hiroshima](../hiroshima.md) · **Index:** [Japan guide index](../README.md)

## Route at a glance

1. Hiroshima Station
2. Shukkei-en
3. Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum area
4. Hiroshima Castle moat
5. Hiroshima Castle grounds
6. A-Bomb Dome
7. Peace Memorial Park
8. Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum
9. Motoyasu River walk
10. Hondori arcade

## The walk

### 1. Hiroshima Station

Begin at Hiroshima Station, where shinkansen, local rail, buses and trams meet.

Use lockers, toilets and food shops here if you are arriving with luggage.

The station is also the easiest place to reset transport plans for Miyajima or the next city.

**Next:** walk 15-20 minutes west, or take a short tram or bus ride, to Shukkei-en.

### 2. Shukkei-en

Shukkei-en is a compact historic garden rebuilt after the war.

Follow the pond edge slowly, using bridges and viewpoints rather than rushing the loop.

Allow 45-60 minutes, especially if the season is good for blossom, iris or autumn leaves.

**Next:** walk 2 minutes to the Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum area.

### 3. Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum area

The museum sits beside Shukkei-en and is useful if an exhibition interests you or the weather turns.

Even without entering, the area provides a calm transition from garden to castle district.

Check current exhibitions and opening days if the museum is a priority.

**Next:** walk 10-12 minutes west towards Hiroshima Castle moat.

### 4. Hiroshima Castle moat

The castle moat gives a broad, green approach to the reconstructed keep.

Pause on the bridges and paths to understand the scale of the former castle grounds.

This is a good place for photos without crowding memorial sites later in the walk.

**Next:** walk 5 minutes inside the grounds towards the castle keep.

### 5. Hiroshima Castle grounds

Hiroshima Castle was reconstructed after the atomic bombing and now explains local history in a park setting.

Enter the keep if you want the displays and views, checking current access first.

Otherwise, spend 30-45 minutes on the grounds and shrine area.

**Next:** walk 15-20 minutes south-west, or take a tram, to the A-Bomb Dome.

### 6. A-Bomb Dome

The A-Bomb Dome is the preserved ruin of the former Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall.

View it from several angles along the river, but keep the tone respectful.

It is most meaningful as part of the wider Peace Memorial Park landscape.

**Next:** cross towards the park; walk 5 minutes to the main memorial axis.

### 7. Peace Memorial Park

The park contains memorials, trees, river views and spaces for remembrance.

Move from the Cenotaph and Flame of Peace towards the museum without treating the route as a checklist.

Allow at least 45 minutes outside before or after the museum.

**Next:** walk 2 minutes along the axis to the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum.

### 8. Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum

The museum presents essential context for the atomic bombing and its human impact.

Plan enough time and take breaks; the visit can be emotionally demanding.

Check current entry procedures, hours and crowd guidance before you go.

**Next:** walk 5 minutes back towards the Motoyasu River after the museum.

### 9. Motoyasu River walk

The river path offers quiet space after the museum and views back to the dome and park.

Walk slowly, sit if needed and avoid loud conversation around memorial areas.

In the evening, the riverside becomes a gentle route towards restaurants.

**Next:** walk 8-10 minutes east to Hondori arcade.

### 10. Hondori arcade

Hondori is the central covered shopping street and an easy place to end the walk.

Use it for coffee, okonomiyaki, shopping or tram connections.

From here, return to Hiroshima Station by tram or continue on foot if you have energy.

**Next:** take a tram from nearby stops to Hiroshima Station, or walk back in about 30 minutes.

## Variations

- **Shorter version:** Take a tram from Hiroshima Station to Genbaku Dome-mae and focus only on the A-Bomb Dome, Peace Memorial Park and museum.
- **Rainy-day version:** Shukkei-en if rain is light, then Prefectural Art Museum, Peace Memorial Museum and Hondori arcade.
- **Evening version:** Hiroshima Castle moat → A-Bomb Dome exterior → riverside walk → Hondori dinner; save the museum for daylight.

## Practical notes

- Toilets are available at Hiroshima Station, Shukkei-en, museum facilities, Peace Memorial Park and Hondori-area shops.
- Coin lockers are easiest at Hiroshima Station; do not rely on finding large lockers near every tram stop.
- Food and coffee stops are best around Hiroshima Station, Hatchobori, Hondori and the streets east of the park.
- The route is mostly flat, with steps possible at castle structures and some pedestrian bridges.
- Trams from Genbaku Dome-mae, Hondori and nearby stops return easily to Hiroshima Station.
- Accessibility is strongest if you use trams and skip optional castle keep stairs.
- Keep voices low around memorials and follow all museum photography rules.
- The museum deserves unhurried time; do not schedule a tight train immediately afterwards.
- Summer heat and rainy-season humidity make indoor breaks important.
