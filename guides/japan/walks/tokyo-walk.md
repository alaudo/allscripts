# Tokyo walking guide

> A coherent central Tokyo walk from the Imperial Palace East Gardens to Marunouchi, Tokyo Station and Ginza; about 5 km, 3-4 hours, easy on mostly flat city pavements.

- **Start:** Otemachi or Takebashi Station **Finish:** Ginza or Higashi-ginza Station **Distance:** ~5 km **Time:** 3-4 hours **Difficulty:** easy
- **City guide:** [Tokyo](../tokyo.md) · **Index:** [Japan guide index](../README.md)

## Route at a glance

1. Otemachi or Takebashi start
2. Imperial Palace moat edge
3. Imperial Palace East Gardens
4. Otemon Gate area
5. Marunouchi office streets
6. Tokyo Station Marunouchi facade
7. Marunouchi Brick Square
8. Yurakucho railway arches
9. Ginza main crossing
10. Kabukiza and Higashi-ginza finish

## The walk

### 1. Otemachi or Takebashi start

This route chooses the Imperial Palace east gardens → Marunouchi/Tokyo Station → Ginza line for a clear central Tokyo cross-section. Start from Otemachi for convenience or Takebashi for a quieter approach by the moat.

**Next:** walk 5-10 minutes to the palace moat edge.

### 2. Imperial Palace moat edge

The water, stone walls and pine trees create a surprisingly calm centre to the city. Office towers rise close by, which makes the historical scale easier to feel.

**Next:** walk 5 minutes to an East Gardens entrance, checking current opening days first.

### 3. Imperial Palace East Gardens

The East Gardens occupy part of the former Edo Castle grounds. Walk slowly through lawns, walls and seasonal plantings rather than treating it as a shortcut.

**Next:** walk 10-15 minutes through the gardens towards the Otemon Gate area.

### 4. Otemon Gate area

The gate gives a strong transition from castle landscape back into business Tokyo. Pause here to look at the stonework and the way the road network bends around the palace grounds.

**Next:** walk 10 minutes into Marunouchi.

### 5. Marunouchi office streets

Marunouchi is polished, ordered and full of lunch spots, boutiques and corporate architecture. It is a useful contrast with the older temple districts and the nightlife areas many visitors see first.

**Next:** walk 5-10 minutes towards Tokyo Station's Marunouchi side.

### 6. Tokyo Station Marunouchi facade

Tokyo Station's restored red-brick facade is one of the city's great railway views. Step inside if you want station food halls, shops or a sense of how many journeys pass through this point.

**Next:** walk 10 minutes south towards Marunouchi Brick Square.

### 7. Marunouchi Brick Square

This small courtyard area gives a human-scaled pause among larger buildings. It is a good place for coffee, toilets and a reset before the retail energy of Ginza.

**Next:** walk 10-15 minutes towards Yurakucho.

### 8. Yurakucho railway arches

Under and beside the tracks, Yurakucho feels more informal than Marunouchi. The restaurants and bars are liveliest later, but even by day the railway infrastructure gives texture to the walk.

**Next:** walk 10 minutes to Ginza's main crossing.

### 9. Ginza main crossing

Ginza is Tokyo in department-store, gallery and showroom mode. Look up at the facades, then choose a side street rather than staying only on the broad avenue.

**Next:** walk 10-15 minutes towards Kabukiza in Higashi-ginza.

### 10. Kabukiza and Higashi-ginza finish

Kabukiza's theatre facade marks a fitting end: traditional performance embedded in a modern commercial district. Check current performance options if you want to return for a single act or longer programme.

**Next:** finish at Higashi-ginza or Ginza Station, or continue on foot towards Tsukiji Outer Market.

## Variations

- **Garden add-on:** Continue from Ginza to Hama-rikyu for a longer green finish near the bay.

- **Food add-on:** Walk from Kabukiza to Tsukiji Outer Market for snacks and kitchenware browsing.

- **Architecture focus:** Spend more time around Marunouchi, Tokyo International Forum and Ginza facades.

- **Rainy-day version:** Use station passages, department stores and cafés to shorten outdoor sections.

## Practical notes

- Check current opening days for the Imperial Palace East Gardens before setting out.

- Weekday lunch hours in Marunouchi are busy; eat early or late if you dislike queues.

- Ginza is especially pleasant in late afternoon and early evening.

- Station exits at Tokyo and Ginza can be confusing; follow signs carefully.

- The route is flat, but comfortable shoes still matter because hard pavements add up.

- Public toilets are easiest in stations, gardens and department stores.
